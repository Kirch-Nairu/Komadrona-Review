# Module Brief — OBS-ANTENATAL-ASSESSMENT

Status: Source acquisition  
Domain: Obstetrics

## 1. Module purpose

This module will provide the structured assessment foundation needed before Komadrona Review publishes detailed antenatal teaching, risk, referral, labor, or postpartum material.

The module is not yet cleared for drafting. Its scope and lesson boundaries are mapped, but its current Philippine source packet remains incomplete.

## 2. Examination-map relationship

Official weighted area:

- Prenatal, Labor and Postpartum Care — 58% of the referenced Obstetrics and Gynecology Table of Specifications

Mapped competency statements:

- Prenatal care and health teaching — 28 items

The module's 12-question target is an internal Komadrona allocation within the 58-question area. It is not presented as an official PRC subtopic allocation.

## 3. Planned lesson units

1. `OBS-ANTENATAL-ASSESSMENT-L01` — Purpose and sequence of antenatal assessment
2. `OBS-ANTENATAL-ASSESSMENT-L02` — Maternal history and risk assessment
3. `OBS-ANTENATAL-ASSESSMENT-L03` — General physical assessment
4. `OBS-ANTENATAL-ASSESSMENT-L04` — Abdominal and obstetric examination
5. `OBS-ANTENATAL-ASSESSMENT-L05` — Leopold maneuvers
6. `OBS-ANTENATAL-ASSESSMENT-L06` — Fetal assessment concepts
7. `OBS-ANTENATAL-ASSESSMENT-L07` — Documentation and continuity of care

## 4. Learning outcomes to refine after source acquisition

The completed module should enable the learner to:

- explain the purpose and broad sequence of routine antenatal assessment;
- identify the information required in a structured maternal history;
- distinguish routine findings from information requiring further evaluation or referral;
- explain the purpose and boundaries of general, abdominal, and obstetric assessment;
- describe the purpose and sequence of Leopold maneuvers at an educational-review level;
- explain the role and limitations of fetal assessment concepts included in the verified source packet;
- document findings and communicate continuity-of-care needs; and
- recognize when the situation exceeds routine educational discussion and requires current protocol, supervision, or referral.

These outcomes are provisional until the Philippine source packet is complete and exact source locators are assigned.

Required evidence:

## 7. Source-reconciliation requirements

Before drafting:

1. Philippine law and current Philippine policy control Philippine-specific practice.
2. WHO guidance may supplement missing explanation but must not silently replace Philippine requirements.
3. Any disagreement in terminology, schedule, threshold, sequence, responsibility, or referral instruction must be recorded.
4. Outdated guidance must be marked rather than blended into current material.
5. No clinical step, threshold, schedule, medication statement, or referral rule may be supplied from memory, AI output, anonymous notes, or commercial reviewer material.

## 8. Drafting boundaries

The first draft must not:

- function as a clinical protocol;
- direct unsupervised patient care;
- state medication doses, treatment orders, or procedural permissions without exact current authority;
- imply that the developer is a midwife, student midwife, or clinical reviewer;
- claim that the module predicts future licensure questions;
- copy examination questions or proprietary reviewer material; or
- use the label `Clinically reviewed` without documented qualified review.

## 9. Required review path

Because this is high-risk clinical educational content, public release requires:

- exact primary-source locators;
- completed source check;
- qualified clinical review;
- recorded reviewer corrections and disposition;
- final editorial and accessibility review; and
- validated linked questions reviewed to the same risk level.

## 10. Assessment plan

Target: 12 original validated questions.

The final set should cover the module's learning objectives using a deliberate mix of:

- understanding;
- application;
- interpretation of structured information; and
- recognition of assessment or referral boundaries.

Every question must include:

- one best answer;
- three plausible but unambiguous distractors;
- answer and distractor rationales;
- exact source locators;
- lesson and competency links;
- difficulty and cognitive-level metadata;
- source-check information; and
- required clinical-review metadata.

Questions will be written only after the corresponding lesson objective has source-checked coverage.
