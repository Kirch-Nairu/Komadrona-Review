**Governance:** OD-001 alone approves five-subject release breadth and progressive verified starter content. This educational concept is **not** an approved clinical curriculum, educational intervention, architecture or published product.

**Purpose:** Design a board-review learning experience for BISU Calape midwifery examinees first and other Philippine Midwives Licensure Examination examinees second, emphasizing understanding, later retrieval, disciplined reasoning, truthful uncertainty and source-backed feedback.

## 1. Why a content map and question bank are insufficient

The project's five exam domains establish what learners may study; they do not establish how a novice acquires a mental model, discriminates between close alternatives, retains information after delay, identifies a misconception, applies concepts in novel cases, or recognizes uncertainty.

The objective is more demanding than page views, quiz attempts or attractive design:

- Explain concepts and terminology accurately with awareness of source scope.
- Retrieve previously learned knowledge without looking at the same explanatory text.
- Discriminate concepts and apply them to **fictional, source-checked** teaching situations.
- Distinguish confidence from correctness and identify when evidence is missing.
- Revisit the right concepts over time, with traceability to content versions.
- Prepare for exam questions using **original, evidence-backed** items without guaranteeing a passing result.

**Critical boundary:** A successful digital exam exercise does not certify safe clinical performance. A website is not clinical placement, hands-on supervised training, patient-specific instruction, professional licensure or independent practice authorization.

## 2. Scientific evidence register (qualitative assessment, not a formal GRADE rating)

| Candidate method | Relevant evidence and limitations | Consequence for concept | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice / self-testing | Dunlosky et al. (2013) evaluated practice testing positively in general education. Trumble et al. (2023) identified 56 health-professions studies / 63 experiments, and 43 studies with significant benefits of retrieval and/or distributed practice, but interventions/outcomes were heterogeneous | Require attempts to recall or reason before showing the answer; avoid counting passive rereading as demonstrated understanding | Strong general basis; **transfer to Komadrona not tested** |
| Distributed/spaced review | Martinengo et al. (2024) found a small pooled knowledge advantage for spaced vs massed online education (SMD 0.32, 95% CI 0.13–0.51, I-squared 66% for nine contributing studies). Retention studies were limited. | Support revisiting across separate study occasions rather than a single cramming session | Supported; **no established optimal interval schedule for MLE topics** |
| Feedback after attempts | Work on test-enhanced learning and formative MCQs suggests corrective explanations help learners understand errors, though length and timing effects vary | Explain the keyed response, the defensibility of distractors, the underlying concept, and a constructive next step | Evidence-informed; **not a guarantee that longer feedback is better** |
| Worked examples and gradual guidance | Cognitive load theory in medical education supports lowering unnecessary load for novices and progressively fading instructional support as competence grows | Teach a concept and worked example before confronting a novice with a complicated vignette; provide optional refreshers | Theory-informed and context-dependent |
| Case-based clinical reasoning | Undergraduate clinical-reasoning reviews favor active, often case-based instruction over passive-only methods, with variation in study quality, student level and outcomes | Include carefully verified fictional applications and contrast cases for topics that genuinely support this mode | Promising; not a substitute for skills assessment or clinical supervision |
| Interleaving | Research suggests mixing related, discriminable problem types can help in some contexts; benefits vary by task and prior knowledge | Mix previously learned **confusable** topics; do not randomly scramble unrelated subjects before basics are known | Conditional option, not a universal requirement |
| Confidence and metacognition | Medical student studies show confidence and correctness are not synonymous; self-assessment is imperfect | Optional sure / somewhat sure / unsure, and gentle review of confident errors or uncertain correct answers | Experimental design option, **not a competence score** |
| Accessibility / learner agency | CAST's Universal Design for Learning Guidelines 3.0 (2024) offers a framework for engagement, representation, action and expression | Provide readable text, navigation and accessible alternatives; allow different paths without labeling fixed learning types | Educational framework, not a measured treatment effect |

## 3. Proposed teaching pattern: EXPLAIN → RECALL → REASON → REVISIT → REFLECT

**This is a candidate pedagogical cycle, not a mandatory screen order or accepted implementation.**

| Stage | What the learner does | Design obligation | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Explain** | Encounters one defined objective, concise source-backed teaching, key terms, a worked example, and appropriate contrast | Match guidance to prerequisite knowledge and objective; make source scope visible | Wall of text or unsupported clinical instructions |
| **Recall** | Attempts a prompt or original question without the answer displayed; can say "I don't know" | Give meaningful feedback and links to the underlying concept | Immediate recognition treated as mastery |
| **Reason** | Applies concept to a fictional, educational case, differentiates plausible alternatives and limitations | Verify all facts, the response key, applicability, professional scope and rationale | Unsourced medical procedure or scenario disguised as safe clinical guidance |
| **Revisit** | Reviews eligible previously learned material after a delay; may mix related objectives | Version-pin items and respect withdrawn/replaced content; let learner control pace | Daily-streak pressure or an unvalidated claim that precise intervals maximize recall |
| **Reflect** | Compares own answer and certainty with evidence, records what to revisit and why | Be supportive and specific; reflect uncertainties rather than manufacturing pass prediction | Grade shame, fake clinical competence badges and invented "board pass probability" |

**Progression:** A novice may need explanation and an example before cases. A repeat learner can enter via retrieval or comparison. No mandatory inflexible funnel is approved; user choice and meaningful prerequisites both matter.

## 4. Proposed presentation — the actual learning experience

### 4.1 Subject library

All five verified subject headings remain visible under OD-001: Obstetrics; Fundamentals of Health Care; Infant Care and Feeding; Primary Health Care; Professional Growth and Development.

Each subject page distinguishes **published/verified, planned, under verification, unknown mapping, corrected/withdrawn**. A planned question or module is not completion; a subject with no released clinical items must say so plainly. Document the exact dated PRC publication associated with alignment rather than inventing TOS subitems.

The available activity paths may be **Learn**, **Practice**, **Apply** and **Revisit**, but only when verified eligible content actually exists. These names are discussion placeholders, not approved navigation UI.

### 4.2 Inside a lesson

A credible lesson should contain **each element only where the learning objective warrants it**:

1. Objective phrased as a demonstrable learner action, linked to an exact subject/competency version or marked subject-only.
2. Necessary prior knowledge and an optional quick refresher.
3. Clear explanation divided by concepts, with claim-level links to named source versions/pages.
4. Worked, **fictional** example, with explicit boundaries for what the source establishes.
5. Common misconception or contrasting idea, itself verified.
6. Low-stakes recall prompt **before** revealing the answer.
7. Response-specific correction: why the answer is supported and why close alternatives are less defensible.
8. Optional, source-checked fictional application/case. Some topics (e.g. terminology or document status) need no clinical vignette.
9. Revisit option tied to the learning objective, exact content version and any correction notices.
10. Source drawer with publisher, edition, locator, verification date, applicable scope and a content-error reporting route.

**Illustrative structure, NOT a real clinical lesson:**

    SUBJECT / objective and prior knowledge
    Why this topic matters → Explain the concept
    Worked example → Contrast a misconception
    Recall without looking → Evidence-backed correction
    Guided case when appropriate → Revisit later
    Source details / correction history

**No fabricated dose, clinical answer, case management, treatment or procedure text is authorized by this outline.**

### 4.3 Inside a study session

A short optional session may open with a delayed review of a previous objective, then one focused lesson, a recall attempt, feedback and next-step selection. The often-mentioned "15–25 minute lesson" is an **unverified UX suggestion**; neither duration nor question count is an empirical optimum here. Learners must be able to pause, choose their path, or skip an optional study reminder.

An assessment session and a learning session should not be conflated. A later timed mock-exam mode, if chosen, would be a distinct nonofficial format, not a clinical qualification or guaranteed licensure prediction.

## 5. Assessment design needs its own pedagogy

### 5.1 Question formats, proposed

| Format | What it could assess | Special review burden |
|---|---|---|
| Very short answer / active recall | Retrieve concept without recognition cues | Exact acceptable-answer criteria; automated grading may be unreliable |
| Original single-best-answer MCQ | Distinguish plausible alternatives | Evidence for keyed answer AND each distractor explanation |
| Contrast/sort/classify | Recognize discriminating features | Objective criteria and precise source scope |
| Fictional stepwise case | Apply and justify knowledge | Scenario premise, role limits, source versions, ambiguity and decision rules verified |
| Source interpretation | Read what a PRC/DOH/CHED/PhilHealth text actually says | Correct edition, jurisdiction, purpose and page/section |
| Optional confidence response | Identify uncertain or overconfident knowledge | No punitive certainty scoring or unsupported clinical safety inference |

**A question is a teaching unit only if feedback actually helps explain the reasoning.** One correct click is insufficient evidence of durable learning. Every item must be original, versioned, competency-linked where verified, and meet the G0 contract's source evidence and rollback/withdrawal rules.

### 5.2 Feedback sequence

Suggested order: show what was selected, identify the claim at issue, present the defensible correct response and concise explanation, address alternative choices with evidence, point to a supporting exact source, offer focused revisit. A question with a disputed key/clinical source is **withdrawn**, not quietly kept active for "practice."

Students who chose a correct option with low confidence may need retrieval practice or explanation; students who chose an incorrect option with high confidence may need misconception correction. Neither result establishes their capacity to treat patients.

## 6. Spacing, progress and psychological safety

- Preserve objective/version for every future practice attempt and correction. The storage mechanism, accounts, offline mode and sync remain undecided.
- Include delayed retrieval after one or more separate sessions, but **do not claim a fixed 1/3/7-day or adaptive algorithm is proven optimal**. The schedule is a choice to evaluate, not a fact.
- Define objective-level evidence carefully. Completion, score, confidence and time spent are distinct measures; none independently proves long-term competence.
- Avoid daily-streak guilt, leaderboards designed to shame, false "mastered" stickers after one question, or pass-probability percentages without validated models.
- A correction/withdrawal event must remove the affected version from active study/review even when a historical quiz attempt or old release exists.
- Do not collect patient information. If future learner progress is stored, privacy, recovery and scope must be explicitly designed.

## 7. Health-professions-specific instructional paths

A generic quiz template cannot serve every subject equally.

- **Anatomy / foundational science:** mechanism, relationships, labeled *licensed* explanatory diagrams when needed, contrast and recall.
- **Clinical theory and professional scope:** carefully sourced conditions, red flags *only if verified*, worked educational case, explain uncertainty and legal scope; never authorize unsupervised action.
- **Midwifery law and ethics:** exact primary statute or Board issuance, effective date, rule versus interpretation, fictional ethics scenario, jurisdiction.
- **Community/public-health programs:** identify whether a source is policy, guidance, implementation procedure, program listing or payer rule before applying it.
- **Procedural concepts:** theory or educational anatomy alone is not skill competence. Professional scope and supervised practical instruction are external to the site.
- **Cross-subject links:** only when evidence and prerequisites justify a link; missing official detailed competencies remain visibly unmapped.

The existing antenatal seven-lesson **research** plan could be used later to critique this instructional architecture, but no lesson is clinically complete; Leopold technique and folate-unit issues are explicitly unresolved. Do not translate research outlines into publishable instruction without exact verification.

## 8. Accessibility and presentation requirements to decide

- Proposed target: WCAG 2.2 AA, mobile/keyboard/screen-reader compatible, scalable text, sensible typography, high contrast, reduced motion, no color-only right/wrong and no essential audio/video-only teaching. **No claim of current compliance.**
- Protect low-bandwidth learners: essential explanation and feedback should function as text, even if diagrams, audio or animation are later included.
- Language remains an owner decision: keep clinically/legal terms correct; bilingual explanations may help but require qualified terminological checking. Do not invent Cebuano/Filipino legal or medical equivalences automatically.
- Avoid bogus "visual learner" / "auditory learner" labeling: Pashler et al. found no adequate basis for matching learning modes to a personality-type classification. Different **representations** can still support accessibility and objective needs.
- Use only licensed or permissioned assets and fictional examples. No university/regulator seals or false endorsement, no clinical-review badge without actual review.

## 9. Proposed educational quality review — additional to source verification

The source/publication contract prevents unsupported clinical statements, but a sourced textbook excerpt is not automatically a good learning activity. Evaluate each instructional unit on:

| Proposed check | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|
| EDU-01 Outcome alignment | Explicit objective and verifiable subject/competency relationship |
| EDU-02 Content accuracy and scope | Claim-level primary-source support, appropriate source version, PH applicability and clinical/professional boundaries |
| EDU-03 Cognitive accessibility | Appropriate prerequisites, examples, explanation length/structure and minimal irrelevant load |
| EDU-04 Active learning | Real recall/reasoning attempt prior to corrective reveal, not only passive scrolling |
| EDU-05 Assessment quality | Original unambiguous task, supported answer criteria and distractor rationales, appropriate challenge |
| EDU-06 Retention support | Revisit mechanism proposed and linked to versions; no unsupported "mastered" claims |
| EDU-07 Inclusion / dignity | Accessible navigation, nonpunitive uncertainty and no coercive streak requirement |
| EDU-08 Valid measurement | Scores describe observed attempts only; no pass-probability or clinical competence claims |
| EDU-09 Correction protection | Source updates and withdrawn versions flow through learning paths and historical attempts |

**Illustrative future checks (NOT EXECUTED):** a beginner can access a worked example; a learner may choose "I don't know"; confidently wrong answer receives constructive feedback; a revision prompt is attempted before revealing the explanation; an absent source blocks publishing; a withdrawn question cannot be reinstated by restoring an old manifest; fixture questions never count as exam coverage; learning remains navigable without mouse or animated content.

## 10. How to evaluate the concept without claiming effectiveness

1. **Inspect instructional coherence:** objective → explanation → recall → worked example/application → feedback → revisit → sources; challenge any decorative exercise without learning purpose.
2. **Check item/content validity:** independent source check, clinically sensitive boundaries, unambiguous stem/key/distractors and actual evidence for each explanation. No mandatory clinical reviewer is added by this concept, but a qualified review label must always reflect real documented review.
3. **Evaluate usability and understanding:** when appropriate and authorized, try representative task walkthroughs and identify ambiguity. Interviews/pilot/demand research are not compulsory prerequisites to *draft* a concept.
4. **Measure actual learning only if evaluation is later authorized:** compare delayed recall and transfer with credible controls/equal time-on-task and defensible matched items; track uncertainty and correction errors. Do not infer impact from clicks, engagement or tiny uncontrolled samples.
5. **Protect dignity and privacy:** do not interpret lower scores as unsafe people or treat higher scores as clinicians.
