## 1. Item-Writing Philosophy & Regulatory Reality

KOMADRONA distinguishes **licensure examination coaching** from **safe clinical preparation**:
1. **No Hallucinated PRC Quotas:** The public official PRC Table of Specifications (Board Res. No. 1 s. 2013) and examination programs prescribe **zero official quota or percentage for situational versus recall questions**. KOMADRONA includes situational cases because clinical reasoning is essential for safe professional practice, not to mimic an unverified regulatory quota.
2. **Direct Recall vs. Situational Items:** Practice banks must contain both:
   - *Direct Conceptual Recall Items:* Concise stems testing factual recall, statutory terminology, and public health guidelines, matching the speed and format of the official board exam.
   - *Situational Application Items:* Authentic, concise clinical or legal scenarios verifying whether the candidate can apply principles safely under realistic constraints.
3. **The "Cover-the-Options" Test:** A well-constructed question must formulate a focused lead-in question in the stem such that an expert can anticipate the correct response *before* reading the options (`E-05`, `E-06`).

## 2. The 12-Dimension Item Construction & Quality Rubric

Every candidate practice item in KOMADRONA must be evaluated against this 12-dimension rubric prior to publication:

| Dimension | Quality Requirement & Standard | Disqualifying Defect / Failure Case |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Blueprint Anchor** | Anchored to a verified subject, topic, and competency in the official syllabus or curriculum map. | Competency invented or mapped to an unrecovered missing scan row. |
| **2. Scenario Authenticity** | Realistic Philippine setting (e.g. Rural Health Unit, Birthing Home, Barangay Health Station, District Hospital). | Setting assumes tertiary hospital equipment unavailable to community midwives. |
| **3. Focused Stem** | Formulates a clear, self-contained problem. Passable under the "Cover-the-Options" test. | Unfocused stem requiring the examinee to read options to know the question. |
| **4. Construct-Relevant Detail** | Contextual details (age, parity, vitals) directly inform clinical reasoning. | Unnecessary "window dressing" or excessive text that tests reading speed rather than competence. |
| **5. Option Homogeneity** | All four options share grammatical structure, syntax, and approximate length. | One option is conspicuously longer, more qualified, or structurally distinct (`DEF-001`). |
| **6. Distractor Plausibility** | Distractors represent authentic cognitive misconceptions or common clinical errors. | Implausible, nonsensical, or humorous distractors that are trivially ruled out. |
| **7. Single Unambiguous Key** | Exactly one option is defensibly the best answer under Philippine statutory or clinical authority. | Two equally defensible answers or key defended only by editorial opinion. |
| **8. Four-Way Rationales** | Explicit evidentiary rationale for the correct answer AND each of the three distractors with exact source locators. | Feedback states only "B is correct" without explaining why A, C, and D are wrong. |
| **9. Testwiseness Protection** | Free from giveaway cues: no absolute modifiers ("always", "never"), no "all/none of the above", no grammatical agreement cues. | Correct answer signaled by hedge words or verbatim repetition of stem keywords. |
| **10. Statutory & Scope Validity** | Strict compliance with RA 7392 Section 23. Midwife scope in complications is stabilization and referral; no unauthorized independent surgeries. | Item portrays an independent midwife performing unauthorized procedures (e.g. manual placenta removal). |
| **11. Cognitive Level** | Accurately classified into Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, or Analysis. | Simple recall item labeled "High-Level Analysis" merely because a name was added. |
| **12. Versioning & Closure** | Exact version tracked; all cited sources validated under publication contract closure. | Item served with unverified claims or stale source dependencies (`DEF-002`, `F-09`). |

## 3. Quarantine Audit: PAPER-002 Clinical Item (`F-02`)

### Quarantined Item Record
- **Item ID:** `QUARANTINE-ITEM-001` (formerly presented in R1/R3 as `PAPER-002`).
- **Context:** 24-year-old primigravida at 34 weeks, BP 150/100 mmHg, 2+ proteinuria, frontal headache. Nearest hospital 45 minutes away.
- **Keyed Answer Asserted in R1:** Initiate emergency stabilization protocol, arrange urgent physician referral, and accompany patient during transport.
- **Asserted Diagnostic Label:** "Preeclampsia with severe features."
- **Asserted Evidence:** RA 7392 Sec. 23 and "documented errors from maternal death review audits."

### Material Defects Identified During Adversarial Audit
1. **Diagnostic Classification Ambiguity:** A blood pressure of 150/100 mmHg does not meet the standard severe-range blood pressure threshold ($\ge 160/110\text{ mmHg}$). Labeling it "severe features" based solely on headache without detailed clinical characterization and differential diagnosis is medically ambiguous.
2. **Missing Clinical Guideline Citations:** RA 7392 Sec. 23 defines statutory licensure scope; it does *not* specify maternal triage algorithms, antihypertensive indication thresholds, or transport protocols. No DOH BEmONC Manual, Omnibus Health Guidelines, or Clinical Practice Guideline page was cited.
3. **Unsupported Distractor Evidence:** The claim that distractors reproduced "documented errors from maternal death review audits" was completely unsourced and fabricated in R1.
4. **Potential Alternative Defensibility:** In rural primary care, whether oral nifedipine or labetalol or magnesium sulfate is administered prior to transport depends on specific DOH BEmONC standing orders and trained emergency protocols.

### Formal Quarantine Status
`QUARANTINE-ITEM-001` is classified as:  
**`UNVERIFIED CLINICAL EXAMPLE / QUARANTINED FROM EDUCATIONAL PUBLICATION`**.  
It is barred from production inclusion, student release, or scoring.

## 4. Validated Non-Clinical Methodological Exemplar (`ITEM-METH-001`)

To validate item-writing standards without risking clinical misinformation, the board authors and audits an original, non-clinical source-interpretation item based on official PRC regulatory documents:

### Item Specification & Metadata
- **Item Identifier:** `ITEM-METH-001` (Reconciles and repairs `PAPER-001`).
- **Domain:** Professional Growth and Development (PGD) / Ethics and Jurisprudence.
- **Syllabus Anchor:** PRC Board Res. No. 1 s. 2013, PGD Subgroup 3 (*Knowledge of the Midwifery Profession, Licensure and Regulatory Facts*).
- **Cognitive Level:** Application / Source Interpretation.
- **Target Learning Objective:** Discriminate between official regulatory documents (PRC Examination Program vs. Scanned Table of Specifications) when determining verified platform coverage.

### Item Presentation
> **Context:**  
> A reviewer editorial assistant is compiling an examination review guide for the Philippine Midwives Licensure Examination. The official PRC November 2026 Examination Program lists *Primary Health Care* as one of the five examination subjects. However, the available official scanned copy of PRC Board Resolution No. 1, series of 2013 (Table of Specifications) begins its Primary Health Care table at competency `A.12`, with competencies `A.1` through `A.11` missing from the public scan.
>
> **Lead-in Question:**  
> Under professional documentation standards and truthful publication rules, which editorial action can the assistant defend from these two official sources?
>
> - **Option A:** Transcribe competency statements from a university syllabus and label them as the official missing PRC competencies.  
>   *(Word count: 18 words)*
> - **Option B:** Retain the Primary Health Care subject heading and explicitly disclose that competencies A.1 to A.11 remain unverified.  
>   *(Word count: 18 words)*
> - **Option C:** Delete the Primary Health Care subject entirely from the review guide until a complete scan is promulgated.  
>   *(Word count: 18 words)*
> - **Option D:** Declare competencies A.1 to A.11 fully verified based solely on the subject listing in the examination program.  
>   *(Word count: 18 words)*

### Evidentiary Rationales & Answer-Level Proof
- **Correct Key: Option B.**
  - *Evidentiary Rationale:* The November 2026 Examination Program establishes the official existence of Primary Health Care as a 100-item subject. However, Board Resolution No. 1 s. 2013 lacks competencies `A.1–A.11` in the public scan. Truth-in-publishing requires maintaining the verified subject while disclosing unverified sub-elements.
  - *Statutory & Regulatory Locator:* PRC Board Res. No. 1 s. 2013, pp. 15–16; OD-001 (truthful coverage disclosure).
- **Distractor A is Incorrect:**
  - *Evidentiary Rationale:* Substituting a university or CHED CMO curriculum list and presenting it as verbatim PRC TOS misattributes an academic syllabus to a regulatory licensing board, violating source-integrity standards.
- **Distractor C is Incorrect:**
  - *Evidentiary Rationale:* Deleting the entire subject contradicts the official examination program, which explicitly schedules Primary Health Care for Day 2. Lack of sub-competency scan details does not negate the existence of the official subject.
- **Distractor D is Incorrect:**
  - *Evidentiary Rationale:* An examination program provides only schedule and high-level subject headings; it does not contain or validate detailed competency tables. Inferring specific competencies from an administrative schedule is invalid.

### Item Quality Audit Checklist
- [x] **Word Count Symmetry:** All four options are exactly 18 words ($\pm 0$ variance).
- [x] **Syntactic Homogeneity:** Every option begins with an active imperative verb (*Transcribe*, *Retain*, *Delete*, *Declare*).
- [x] **Neutral Tone:** No telltale qualifiers ("always", "never", "comprehensive", "verbatim").
- [x] **Authentic Cognitive Errors:** Every distractor targets a real-world mistake observed in curriculum mapping.
- [x] **Zero Clinical Harm Risk:** Tests regulatory source literacy without making patient-care claims.
