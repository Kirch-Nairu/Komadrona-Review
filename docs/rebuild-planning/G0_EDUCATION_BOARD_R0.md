## Substantive initial tensions to put before the board

These are **questions generated by one author**, not fictitious quotes from B1–B8:

1. **Scaffolding vs exam fidelity:** The real exam experience may withhold hints; novices may need them. Should the learner interface have two distinct modes, and when does it transition?
2. **Situational realism vs cognitive noise:** Longer fictional cases may look realistic but hide that the same concept could be assessed with a shorter, more valid question. How would we test discriminating clues?
3. **Spaced practice vs poor question quality:** Spacing an invalid item propagates a misconception. How must current item/source eligibility constrain every review queue?
4. **Mock percentage vs false confidence:** What can a small, uncalibrated practice bank reliably say about an exam candidate? How do we show progress without pretending to predict passing?
5. **Clinical accuracy vs instructional effectiveness:** An exactly cited statement can still be a poor explanation. What rubric separates the two without imposing new fake clinical-credential requirements?
6. **Educational ambition vs constrained access:** Could immersive interactions disadvantage phone-only students? What text-first alternative retains assessment validity?
7. **Official exam mapping vs hidden blueprint:** How do we honestly design application/analysis/situational practice when exact proportions of item types are not official/public?
8. **Specialist disagreements:** Which proposal should the skeptic reject if educational citations are from medicine/nursing rather than Philippine midwifery?
